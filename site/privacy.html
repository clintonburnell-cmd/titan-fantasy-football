Privacy

What Titan keeps, who it talks to, how long it keeps things, and how to see or delete your data.

Last updated September 15, 2026. Titan is run by Titan Forge, a one-person business, which is the controller of the data described here.

On your device

Titan keeps these in your browser's storage on your own device:

  • The Sleeper username you link, the ESPN leagues you add (league ID and which team is yours), and the league switches you choose.
  • The rankings you import.
  • A copy of your last refresh (rosters, injuries and game locks) so the app opens instantly.
  • A trimmed copy of Sleeper's public player list (names, positions and teams).
  • Your light or dark choice, on the website and in the app.

Clearing your browser data, or tapping Switch account in Settings, removes them from that device.

If you sign in to sync

Syncing is optional. If you sign in with Google, Titan uses Google's Firebase service (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore, stored in the United States) to keep these the same across your devices:

  • Your Google account's name, email address and profile picture, used only to sign you in and show who is signed in.
  • The Sleeper account you linked (username, user ID, display name and profile picture ID), the ESPN leagues you added, and your league switches.
  • The rankings you import, week by week.
  • Your ESPN login, only if you save it for private ESPN leagues: the espn_s2 and SWID cookies ESPN's website uses to keep you signed in.
  • If you turn on game-day alerts: a notification address for each device you turned them on for (a push token from Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging), which alerts you want, which were already sent this week (so none repeats), and the names of the starters Titan is watching for news, so a breaking story can be matched to your teams without re-reading your leagues.

Titan's server uses a saved ESPN login only to read your ESPN leagues, which a browser can't do for a private league itself. The app never shows it again, and Settings → Link more leagues? → ESPN → Remove my ESPN login deletes it. Because those cookies keep you signed in to ESPN, signing out of ESPN on every device also makes a saved copy stop working.

Only you can read or change your synced data: Titan's database rules allow access to a person's data only when that same person is signed in. Titan doesn't share it or sell it, and Titan's owner sees only totals (such as how many accounts exist or how many added an ESPN league), never anyone's details.

While you have a Titan account and have used Titan in the last 45 days, Titan's server also keeps a record of each week: on NFL game days, every 15 minutes, it reads your linked Sleeper and ESPN leagues and your synced rankings, and saves each player's rank, Titan's call and his projected points under your account, frozen at his game's kickoff. The Results tab compares how your teams did with that record. An account that hasn't opened Titan in 45 days is left alone until it comes back, unless it has alerts on.

If you turn on alerts, the same check looks for starters ruled out or on bye and empty lineup spots, and sends an alert to your devices through Firebase Cloud Messaging. On days without games it also looks for players ruled out at noon, 4 PM and 8 PM Eastern, when injury reports come out. Turning alerts off on a device, or signing out there, removes its address.

To delete everything, open Settings → Sync across your devices → Delete my Titan account. That removes your synced data and your Titan sign-in. Signing out stops syncing on that device without deleting anything.

If you join the weekly email

The Titan Waiver Wire is an optional free email, once a week during the season. If you sign up, your email address goes to Kit (kit.com), the email service that keeps the list and sends the emails for Titan. Kit sends a confirmation email first, and every email has an unsubscribe link that takes you off the list. Like most email services, Kit counts which emails are opened and which links are clicked. Titan uses your address only to send this guide and never sells or shares it. Your browser also remembers that you joined, so Titan stops asking.

How long Titan keeps things

  • Synced data stays until you delete your account, or remove a piece of it in Settings.
  • The weekly record of Titan's calls is kept for the current season and the one before, then deleted.
  • Alert addresses go when you turn alerts off on that device or sign out there.
  • Deleted data leaves the live database at once, and Titan's daily backups within 7 days.
  • Google Cloud keeps its own logs of requests to Titan's server (the address of the device and its browser, not who you are) for up to 30 days.

Your rights and how to use them

Wherever you live, you can see, correct, take away or delete what Titan holds about you, and object to how it's used. Most of it you can do yourself: Settings shows what's linked and synced, lets you remove an ESPN login or turn alerts off, and Delete my Titan account removes everything. For a copy of your synced data, a correction, or anything else, email the address under Contact and it will be handled within 30 days. Titan isn't for children under 13, and doesn't knowingly keep anything about them.

Who Titan talks to

  • Sleeper (api.sleeper.app): your device reads your leagues directly, the same information anyone can see on your public Sleeper profile. Titan has no password for your Sleeper account and cannot change your lineups, make claims or trade. Projected points also come from Sleeper, which gets them from RotoWire, and player photos, team logos and avatars load from Sleeper's image server (sleepercdn.com), on the website's home page too.
  • ESPN (fantasy.espn.com): your device reads ESPN's public NFL schedule for kickoff times, which sends nothing about you. If you add ESPN leagues, public ones are read by your device directly and private ones by Titan's server with the ESPN login you saved. When Titan's server can't reach ESPN's scoreboard, your browser reads the live scores from ESPN directly instead. Titan can't change your ESPN lineups either.
  • Google: Firebase, for sign-in and sync if you choose to sign in, for delivering alerts if you turn them on, for hosting the app, and for this week's Waiver Wire on the newsletter page once you've joined, which your browser reads from Titan's Firebase database without sending anything about you.
  • Cloudflare (cloudflareinsights.com): the website's pages count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sees the page opened, the site that linked to it, and your browser, device type and country. It uses no cookies, doesn't know who you are and doesn't follow you to other sites. The app, the Android app and copies on your home screen never load it.
  • Kit (app.kit.com): only if you sign up for the weekly email, as described above.
  • Ko-fi: only if you tap the tip jar on the website. Tips are handled entirely by Ko-fi, and Titan never sees your payment details.
  • X: only if you tap an account on the News tab, which opens it in the X app or on x.com.

Titan's server also reads FantasyCalc (trade values), the National Weather Service (weather at kickoff) and nflverse (defensive stats) to build the numbers everyone sees. Those are read once and shared with every user, and nothing about you is sent to them.

What Titan doesn't do

  • No advertising, and no analytics in the app. The website's visit counts (above) use no cookies and can't tell who you are.
  • No selling or sharing of anything.

If Titan changes hands

If Titan is ever sold or passed to a new owner, the data described here would move with it and stay under this policy. You'd be told before any change to these promises takes effect, and you can delete your data in Settings at any time.
