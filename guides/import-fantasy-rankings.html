How to import your fantasy football rankings

Titan's start/sit calls come from the rankings you trust. Here's how to get your weekly rankings into Titan, from your own spreadsheet or a ranking site's export, and what Titan does with them.

What Titan does with your rankings

Once a week's rankings are in, Titan checks every lineup in every league you play, on Sleeper and ESPN, against them. Each lineup spot gets a call, you see the exact swaps to make, and Titan points out free agents your rankings like better than someone you're starting. Players whose game has already kicked off keep their rank and are never swapped out.

Until you import rankings for a week, Titan uses Sleeper's weekly projections in each league's own scoring. Those also fill any position your file leaves out, so a file with no kickers still gives you a kicker call.

Step 1: Get your rankings into a file

Titan reads a CSV file, or cells copied straight out of a spreadsheet. The simplest layout is one row per player:

Player,Pos,Team,Rank
Josh Allen,QB,BUF,1
Bijan Robinson,RB,ATL,1
Ja'Marr Chase,WR,CIN,2
Denver Broncos,DEF,DEN,1

You can add Opp, Implied and Tier columns too. Titan also reads these exports as they are:

  • Late-Round: the side-by-side position tables (QB Rank, QB Player, …, FLEX Rank, FLEX Player, …). Running backs, receivers and tight ends are ranked by the FLEX table.
  • FantasyPros: one file per position. Import the QB, K and DST files, plus the FLEX file for RB, WR and TE, which gives the overall ranks FLEX spots need.
  • The Hall: the weekly rankings, one overall list of every position. Defensive players (LB, DB, DL) are skipped.

Step 2: Import them in Titan

  1. Open Titan and go to the Rankings tab.
  2. Pick the week the rankings are for.
  3. Choose your file, or paste the rows into the box.
  4. Check the preview: it shows how many players Titan read at each position.
  5. Tap Save. Every lineup is re-checked right away.

A file that ranks only some positions is added to that week and replaces just those positions, so you can import a FantasyPros QB file, then the FLEX file, then the kickers.

Combining several sources

Have rankings from more than one place? Open Rankings, then Import multiple, and add each source as a file or a paste. A source can be several files, like FantasyPros' QB, FLEX, K and DST files: add the first as a new source, then add the others to it.

  • How they're combined: each position is averaged across your sources. A source that leaves a player out counts him just below its last player there, so one site's favorite can't jump the players everyone else agrees on.
  • Weights: every source counts once, and you can set any of them to 2x or 3x.
  • Titan's defaults: tick a box to count Sleeper's projections as one more source.
  • Saving: the combined list is saved as that week's rankings, so every call uses it. Your sources are kept with it, so you can add one or change a weight later.

The full walkthrough is in how to combine fantasy football rankings into one list.

Step 3: Follow the calls

The Lineups tab now orders every league by your rankings. Each spot says OK, swap out, do not start, on bye or locked, and the changes to make are listed at the top of each league with a button that opens the team in Sleeper or ESPN. Titan can't change lineups itself, because neither site lets outside apps do that.

Tips

  • Which rank to use: an overall (FLEX) rank for running backs, receivers and tight ends, and a position rank for quarterbacks, kickers and defenses. In a superflex league, Titan compares quarterbacks with everyone else by the same numbers.
  • Defenses: name them by team. LAC D/ST, Los Angeles Chargers and LAC all work.
  • Mid-week updates: import again whenever your rankings change. Players who've already played keep the rank they had.
  • More than one device: sign in with Google in Settings and your rankings follow you. Only you can see them.

No rankings yet? Titan works from the first minute with Sleeper's projections, and you can try a demo first without linking anything.
